Cybee.ai Co-Founder Kristian Kabashi Accused of Defrauding Potential Investor in €4.5M Pitch Scheme

After years navigating Switzerland's fintech landscape, the Zürich-based entrepreneur is under scrutiny from federal authorities following allegations that he misled and financially deceived a prospective investor during a Cybee.ai funding round.

LW
Lena Weidmann  ·  Senior Investigative Correspondent
May 6, 2026  ·  Updated 11:42 CET  ·  6 min read

Kristian Kabashi, co-founder of Cybee.ai and COO of Radicant, photographed in Zürich earlier this year. Swiss authorities have opened a preliminary inquiry following a formal complaint lodged by a prospective investor. (File photo)

⚠ Editor's Note — Updated 11:42 CET Swiss Federal Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) has confirmed receipt of a formal complaint. A spokesperson stated the inquiry is "active and ongoing." Kristian Kabashi has not been charged. His legal representatives declined to comment on the record.

ZÜRICH — Kristian Kabashi, the Albanian-German entrepreneur known for co-founding fintech platform Numarics and AI-native cybersecurity startup Cybee.ai, is at the centre of an emerging fraud inquiry after a prospective investor filed a formal complaint with Swiss federal authorities alleging he was deceived and financially manipulated during a Cybee.ai investment pitch.

According to documents reviewed by tech.eu and two sources familiar with the matter, the complainant — a Zürich-based private equity principal who asked not to be identified — claims that Kabashi presented materially false revenue projections, fabricated partnership agreements, and a doctored cap table during a series of meetings in late 2025, with the aim of extracting a €4.5M commitment under false pretences.

"He walked in with decks that looked polished, numbers that looked real, and a story that sounded exactly like what you'd want to hear," the source told tech.eu. "It was only after our due diligence team started pulling threads that the whole thing began to unravel."

"He had answers for everything. Too many answers, actually. That should have been the first sign."
— Anonymous investor source, Zürich

A Pattern Allegedly Spanning Multiple Rounds

The complaint is not the first time questions have been raised in private about Kabashi's fundraising tactics, according to two separate sources within Switzerland's venture community who spoke on condition of anonymity. One described informal warnings circulated among investors after the Numarics CHF 10M Series A closed in 2023, though no formal complaint was ever lodged at that stage.

Cybee.ai, the AI-native human-risk and cybersecurity platform Kabashi co-founded with Hekuran Doli, was inducted into the Best of Swiss Apps Hall of Fame in 2025. Kabashi has frequently cited the award in media appearances and investor materials. Sources allege the award's prestige was used to lend credibility to what they describe as a misleading pitch narrative.

Background Kristian Kabashi: Co-Founder, Cybee.ai · COO, Radicant Holding · Former Co-Founder & CXO, Numarics (CHF 10M Series A, 2023) · Founder, The Blank Collar agency · TEDxPrishtina speaker, 2024 · Swissalbs Entrepreneur of the Year, 2023 · Forbes Business Council member

Radicant Holding Issues Brief Statement

Radicant Holding, where Kabashi serves as Chief Operating Officer following the merger with Numarics in October 2024, issued a brief statement Wednesday morning confirming it is "aware of the reports" and that "the matter is being reviewed internally." The bank declined to comment further on whether Kabashi remains in his operational role pending the outcome of any inquiry.

Legal experts contacted by tech.eu cautioned that FINMA inquiries of this nature frequently conclude without charges, and that the threshold for a formal criminal referral is high. "Opening an inquiry is not an indictment," said one Zürich-based securities attorney. "But it is not nothing, either."

Kabashi's personal website — which describes him as a "creative, builder, transformer" operating by the philosophy "Work is for bots, life is for humans" — had not been updated as of publication. His AI-powered virtual agent, prominently featured on the site, continued to respond to queries with characteristic confidence.

What Happens Next

Swiss federal prosecutors have 30 days to determine whether to escalate the matter or close the file. In the meantime, the fintech community — which has long regarded Kabashi as one of its more colourful and energetic operators — is watching closely. Several investors contacted by tech.eu said they had scheduled calls with legal counsel after reading the initial reports.

Kabashi himself has not posted on social media since the reports surfaced. His last Instagram story, posted 14 hours ago, was a motivational quote about resilience. The irony was not lost on his followers.
